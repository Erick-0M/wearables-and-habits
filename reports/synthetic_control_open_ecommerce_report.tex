\section{Summary}
Exploratory unit-level synthetic control of first wearable purchase on running-related purchases, as an alternative to the staggered DiD of \#7 (which fails on pre-trends). Each adopter gets its own synthetic twin from never-treated users, matched on the 8 pre-adoption quarters; gaps are averaged over adopters in event time.
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \textbf{Pre-fit is easy, and removes the pre-trend by construction.} Pre-period gaps are about zero for every outcome (Figures~\ref{fig:main}, \ref{fig:plac}), so the ramp-up into adoption that broke DiD is absorbed by the synthetic twin.
\item \textbf{Post gaps are positive, not specific to running.} Matching on the outcome's own history, the post ATT (quarters 1--8) is $+0.017$ (0.006) for running shoes, $+0.070$ (0.012) running-related, $+0.033$ (0.009) fitness gear, $+0.100$ (0.016) supplements. But the placebo outcomes are positive too (pet $+0.036$, books $+0.057$, grocery $+0.039$) and so is total purchasing, $\ln(1+n)$: $+0.176$ (0.038). Adopters simply buy more of everything afterwards than their synthetic twins.
\item \textbf{Adding $\ln(1+n)$ to the match shrinks the effects} (Table~\ref{tab:lnn}): running shoe $+0.011$ (0.006), running-related $+0.043$ (0.012), supplements $+0.068$ (0.017), and the placebos fall to $+0.018$, $+0.030$, $-0.001$. Running-related and supplements remain above the placebos, but this is suggestive at best.
\item \textbf{Not window-end attrition.} Requiring donors to be observed through the entire post window barely changes anything (e.g.\ running-related $+0.066$ own, $+0.039$ with $\ln n$ match; \texttt{synth\_summary\_fulldonor.csv}).
\item \textbf{Reading.} The synthetic estimates and the \#7 DiD disagree in sign, so neither is credible alone. A plausible driver of the common positive gap is selection of adopters on a purchase in $e=0$ (and on $\geq$12 months of activity after), which donors are not selected on; this predicts positive gaps for all outcomes, as observed. The placebo test with pseudo-adopters drawn from donors (red) is $\approx 0$, but those units lack that selection, so it does not rule this out.
\end{itemize}

\section{Design}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \textbf{Data:} the \#7 user-quarter panel (\texttt{design\_panel\_q.csv}), ``clean'' sample. Treated: clean adopters with 8 observed pre-quarters, i.e.\ adoption in 2020Q1 or later (288 of 447). Donors: never-treated users (about 4{,}100) observed in all 8 pre-quarters.
\item \textbf{Weights:} per adopter, non-negative weights summing to one on the 150 nearest donors (pre-period distance), least squares on the 8 pre-quarters (SLSQP). Spec ``own'' matches the outcome only; ``own$+\ln n$'' also matches $\ln(1+n)$. Outcomes: any purchase in the quarter (as in \#7).
\item \textbf{Effects:} gap$_{i,e}$ = adopter minus synthetic, $e=-8,\dots,8$, $e=0$ the adoption quarter; ATT$_e$ = mean over adopters, SE = cross-adopter SD$/\sqrt{n}$ (ignores weight estimation error). Post ATT = mean of $e=1,\dots,8$ (excludes the device quarter).
\item \textbf{Placebo:} 265 never-treated users get randomly drawn real adoption quarters and are run through the same procedure against the remaining donors (Placebo column, red points).
\item Code: \texttt{code/scripts/synth\_control.py}, \texttt{synth\_control\_figs.py}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Caveats and follow-ups}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item Treated sample is the later adopters only (2020+), so not comparable to \#7's 447.
\item Follow-ups (not done): hold-out pre-period fit to test overfitting; synthetic DiD / augmented SC with a unit fixed effect; pseudo-adopters selected on an $e=0$ purchase to test the selection explanation; not-yet-treated donors; HonestDiD on \#7.
\end{itemize}
